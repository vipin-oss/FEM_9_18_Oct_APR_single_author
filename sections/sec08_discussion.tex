\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The directionality of the separations in Case~H are due to the anisotropic gradient stiffness of a homogeneous continuum. The two frequencies on the ends of the volume-equivalent length will move by different amounts for a change in aspect ratio. The propagation directions that are most affected are different when the rotation is changed. Where $\Delta_{GX}>0$ the complete-gap measure is negative $\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}}\le -0.3235$. No material difference and configuration 42 is a finite design grid. The results are for reduced-zone dispersion. They are not about a Bragg attenuation gap or continuous optimized design.

Another expression of the same anisotropy is obtained from the group velocity calculation. In the acoustic branch at $\bar{k}=0.5$ rotation causes the steering lobes to rotate about the constant-wavenumber ring. The angular deviation is not significantly affected by their angular deviation. The local normal to a contour of constant frequency, $\bmv_g$, is equal to $\nabla_{\bmk}\omega$. This direction does not necessarily have to be along or against the computed steering, which is along $\bmk$. This result is applicable to the branch and wavenumber ring test.

The case~C is different, since its shear modulus and density are periodically changing. Bands~3 and~4 stay separated over the sampled Brillouin zone. The complete-gap width falls from $2.5732$ on the $4\times4$ mesh to $1.9179$ on the $64\times64$ mesh. The width needs to be further refined before it can be used as a continuous variable. There are differences between the homogeneous and composite cases too in their material and numerical settings. The comparison thus reveals two mechanisms. Does not single out one parameter.

Numerical checks are used to back up any implementation within these limits. The empirical rate of the homogeneous mesh study is $p=4.17$ for the first three resolutions and the result at $32^2$ is at the numerical floor. There are 8 consistency checks that meet their criteria. One of them is the closed-form Case-H residual of energy-flux, $6.91\times10^{-10}$. The classical relation between AlN and BaTiO$_3$ transfer matrix fits well with 2.22×10$^{-16}$. The comparisons with published bilayer band plots remain qualitative due to the absence of source data or conventions for the parameters. For the transverse-branch asymptotics, the micro-inertia term is equal to the gradient-stiffness term, and provides a finite phase speed at high wavenumber. The other branches are not included in this result. The continuum model is linear, local, isothermal and two dimensional. Its effects do not account for dissipation, nonlinear effects, piezoelectric coupling or the three dimensional polarisation modes.
