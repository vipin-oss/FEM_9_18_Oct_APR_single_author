\noindent Dear Prof.\ Wriggers,

I respectfully submit the manuscript entitled \textbf{``Orientation-Dependent Dispersion and Wave Steering in Two-Dimensional Strain-Gradient Elasticity: Directional Spectral Separation versus Complete Bragg Gaps under Material Contrast''} for consideration as an Original Research Article in \textit{Computational Mechanics}.

\textbf{Computational formulation and verification.}
Higher-order strain-gradient elastodynamics requires $C^1$-continuous displacement fields together with phase-consistent periodic coupling of both displacements and spatial derivatives. In this manuscript, I formulate and verify a two-dimensional Bloch--Floquet finite-element framework combining a rotated ellipsoidal characteristic-length tensor $\bm{L}(\theta,\mathrm{AR})$, local Mindlin Form-II strain-gradient elasticity, and independent gradient micro-inertia, discretized with a conforming 32-degree-of-freedom Bogner--Fox--Schmit bicubic Hermite element. Bloch phase factors are enforced across unit-cell boundaries on the full Hermite nodal state (values, first derivatives, and mixed second derivatives), and branch continuity across symmetry crossings is tracked using a micro-inertia-weighted Modal Assurance Criterion. An eight-part verification suite confirms machine-precision Hermiticity, reciprocal-lattice periodicity, orientation/swap symmetries, closed-form dispersion recovery with an empirical convergence rate $p = 4.17$ down to eigensolver reproducibility, bounded high-wavenumber phase speeds under micro-inertia, and agreement between central-difference group velocity and cycle-averaged higher-order Poynting energy flux to $6.91\times 10^{-10}$.

\textbf{Principal findings and numerical transparency.}
The study separates wave phenomena governed by tensor orientation in a homogeneous strain-gradient continuum from those requiring periodic material contrast:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item In the homogeneous medium (Case~H), rotating and elongating $\bm{L}(\theta,\mathrm{AR})$ produces a directional spectral separation along $\Gamma$--$X$ (up to $0.0677$ at $\theta=0^\circ$, $\mathrm{AR}=7$), whereas the complete-gap metric remains strictly negative across all 42 sampled $(\theta,\mathrm{AR})$ configurations, establishing that homogeneous directional separation is a reduced-zone folding property rather than Bragg attenuation.
  \item On the acoustic branch, rotation of the characteristic-length tensor steers the cycle-averaged energy flux relative to the wave vector by up to $2.7846^\circ$ ($\mathrm{AR}=10$), with the peak steering angle remaining invariant to within $10^{-5}$ degrees across orientation.
  \item Introducing periodic epoxy--YBCO material contrast (Case~C) opens a positive complete Bragg band gap across all tested meshes ($4\times 4$ through $64\times 64$, up to $33{,}800$ DOFs). Because the circular inclusion interface cuts through structured Cartesian elements without interface-fitted sub-triangulation, the complete gap narrows monotonically from $2.5732$ ($4\times 4$) to $1.9179$ ($64\times 64$) with successive decrement ratios of $0.552$--$0.565$ (Supplementary Material, Table~S4). Both this non-fitted quadrature limitation and the resulting mesh-unconverged gap sequence are explicitly disclosed.
\end{enumerate}
