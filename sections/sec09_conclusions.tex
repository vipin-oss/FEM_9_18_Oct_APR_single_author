\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The main findings from the two-dimensional Mindlin Form-II Bloch-wave calculations are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The conforming Bogner--Fox--Schmit element applies the same Bloch phase to displacement and derivative degrees of freedom. Analytical limits and numerical checks support the implementation.

  \item In homogeneous Case~H, the tensor orientation and aspect ratio shift the dispersion branches. Directional reduced-zone separations appear. We find no complete band gap.

  \item At fixed ellipse area, the separation does not vary monotonically with aspect ratio. The largest sampled value is $\Delta_{GX}=0.0677$ at $\theta=0^\circ$ and $\mathrm{AR}=7$.

  \item Rotating the length tensor changes the direction of acoustic energy flow. The peak group-velocity deviation is about $2.78^\circ$, and it hardly changes across the sampled orientations.

  \item The periodic epoxy--YBCO composite shows a gap between Bands~3 and~4 on all computed meshes. The width decreases under refinement and is not converged.

  \item Gradient micro-inertia limits the phase speed of the transverse branch at high wavenumber to $0.3162$ in normalised units. Without micro-inertia, this phase speed is unbounded.
\end{itemize}

These results are relevant to direction-dependent elastic-wave transport in microstructured solids and to phononic media with controlled wave steering. The homogeneous model isolates orientation effects, since it has no periodic material contrast. The composite calculations address band-gap formation. Practical use needs material-specific calibration and further convergence checks for the composite gap.
