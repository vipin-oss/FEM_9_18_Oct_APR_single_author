
\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The two-dimensional Mindlin Form-II Bloch-wave analysis leads to the following main findings:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The conforming Bogner--Fox--Schmit element applies the same Bloch phase to displacement and derivative degrees of freedom. Analytical limiting cases and numerical checks support the implementation.

    \item In homogeneous Case~H, the dispersion branches change with the orientation and aspect ratio of the characteristic-length tensor. Directional reduced-zone separations are observed, but no complete band gap is found.

    \item The directional separation is not monotonic with the aspect ratio at a fixed ellipse area. The largest sampled value is $\Delta_{GX}=0.0677$ at $\theta=0^\circ$ and $\mathrm{AR}=7$.

    \item When changing the orientation of the characteristic-length tensor, the direction of the acoustic energy flow changes. The group-velocity deviation is at most about $2.78^\circ$ and changes only slightly with the various orientations sampled.

    \item For the periodic epoxy--YBCO composite, the computed meshes all show a gap between Bands~3 and~4. Convergence has not been proved, however, the gap width is shrinking with mesh refinement.
    \item For high wavenumbers, the transverse-branch phase speed is capped by gradient micro-inertia at normalised speed of $0.3162$. If there is no gradient micro-inertia, the phase speed increases indefinitely.
\end{itemize}

The results presented herein are for direction dependent elastic wave transport and steering in strain gradient solids. The homogeneous model treats the effect of the orientation of the tensor but does not incorporate periodic material contrast. The composite model separately examines band-gap formation due to periodic heterogeneity. Further mesh-convergence studies and material-specific calibration are required before quantitative predictions of the composite band gap can be made.
