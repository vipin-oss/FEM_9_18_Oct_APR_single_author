\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

For homogeneous Case~H, rotating the length tensor changes the gradient stiffness seen by waves travelling in different directions. The frequency shifts are unequal, so neighboring bands may separate along $\Gamma$--$X$ while their ranges still overlap elsewhere. Varying aspect ratio at fixed volume-equivalent length also changes the two band edges differently. This accounts for the non-monotone directional separation. The complete-gap measure remains negative ($\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}}\le -0.3235$), even where $\Delta_{GX}>0$. The separation is a reduced-zone feature of the homogeneous continuum, not an attenuation gap. The 42 sampled designs do not establish a continuous optimum.

The steering result can be understood from the constant-frequency contours. Their local normals give $\bmv_g=\nabla_{\bmk}\omega$; rotation of the length tensor changes those normals and hence the energy-flow direction. At $\bar{k}=0.5$, the steering lobes rotate while their peak angular deviation changes very little across the tested orientations. This observation is restricted to the sampled acoustic branch and wavenumber ring.

In Case~C, the shear modulus and density vary periodically. Bands~3 and~4 have a positive complete-gap measure over the sampled Brillouin zone, whereas the homogeneous Case~H exhibits only path-specific separation. The Case-C gap decreases from $2.5732$ on the $4\times4$ mesh to $1.9179$ on the $64\times64$ mesh and remains unconverged. Because the cases use different material and discretization settings, the comparison is interpretive rather than a controlled parameter sweep; the coarse-mesh Case-C width is not a continuum estimate.

The mesh study gives $p=4.17$ from the first three resolutions. The $32^2$ point is resolution-limited and was not included. This estimate describes the observed convergence, not a proven element order. All eight tabulated numerical consistency checks meet their stated criteria; the energy-flux/group-velocity identity, evaluated on closed-form Case-H harmonic fields, holds to $6.91\times10^{-10}$.

For the classical AlN/BaTiO$_3$ bilayer, the transfer-matrix relation is reproduced to $2.22\times10^{-16}$. Comparison with the published B1 curves remains visual because their underlying numerical values are unavailable. Unresolved parameter conventions in the two gradient-elastic bilayer sources prevent quantitative external validation.

For the transverse branch derived in Appendix~\ref{app:asymptotics}, gradient stiffness causes unbounded high-wavenumber phase speed if micro-inertia is omitted. Including micro-inertia adds a matching $k^2$ term to the denominator of $v_p^2$, yielding a finite limit determined by the two length scales. This is a mechanism-specific result for the branch and constitutive model studied, not a universal bound. More broadly, the model is linear, local, isothermal, and two-dimensional under plane-strain assumptions; it excludes nonlinear gradient effects, dissipation, piezoelectric coupling, and three-dimensional polarization modes.
