\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The directional separations in Case~H come from the anisotropic gradient stiffness of a homogeneous continuum. At fixed volume-equivalent length, a change in aspect ratio shifts the two bounding frequencies by different amounts. Rotation changes which propagation directions are affected most. Even where $\Delta_{GX}>0$, the complete-gap measure is negative ($\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}}\le -0.3235$). There is no material contrast, and the 42 configurations sample a finite design grid. The results describe reduced-zone dispersion. They do not describe a Bragg attenuation gap or a continuously optimized design.

The group-velocity calculation gives a second view of the same anisotropy. On the acoustic branch at $\bar{k}=0.5$, rotation moves the steering lobes around the constant-wavenumber ring. Their maximum angular deviation changes little. The local normal to a constant-frequency contour gives $\bmv_g=\nabla_{\bmk}\omega$. Its direction need not coincide with $\bmk$, which is the origin of the computed steering. The result holds for the branch and wavenumber ring tested.

Case~C behaves differently because its shear modulus and density vary periodically. Bands~3 and~4 stay separated over the sampled Brillouin zone. The complete-gap width falls from $2.5732$ on the $4\times4$ mesh to $1.9179$ on the $64\times64$ mesh. Further refinement is needed before the width can be taken as a continuum value. The homogeneous and composite cases also differ in material and numerical settings. The comparison therefore shows two different mechanisms. It does not isolate a single parameter.

Numerical checks support the implementation within these limits. The homogeneous mesh study gives an empirical rate of $p=4.17$ on its first three resolutions, and the $32^2$ result lies at the numerical floor. Eight consistency checks meet their criteria. One of them is a $6.91\times10^{-10}$ energy-flux/group-velocity residual from closed-form Case-H fields. The classical AlN/BaTiO$_3$ transfer-matrix relation agrees to $2.22\times10^{-16}$. Comparisons with published bilayer band plots stay qualitative because source data or parameter conventions are missing. In the transverse-branch asymptotics, micro-inertia balances the gradient-stiffness contribution and gives a finite phase speed at high wavenumber. Other branches are not covered by this result. The continuum model is linear, local, isothermal and two-dimensional. Dissipation, nonlinear effects, piezoelectric coupling and three-dimensional polarization modes are not included.
