\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

For homogeneous Case~H, rotating the length tensor changes the gradient stiffness seen by waves travelling in different directions. The frequency shifts are unequal, so neighboring bands may separate along $\Gamma$--$X$ while their ranges still overlap elsewhere. Varying aspect ratio at fixed volume-equivalent length also changes the two band edges differently. This accounts for the non-monotone directional separation. The complete-gap measure remains negative ($\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}}\le -0.3235$), even where $\Delta_{GX}>0$. The separation is a reduced-zone feature of the homogeneous continuum, not an attenuation gap. The 42 sampled designs do not establish a continuous optimum.

The steering result can be understood from the constant-frequency contours. Their local normals give $\bmv_g=\nabla_{\bmk}\omega$; rotation of the length tensor changes those normals and hence the energy-flow direction. At $\bar{k}=0.5$, the steering lobes rotate while their peak angular deviation changes very little across the tested orientations. This observation is restricted to the sampled acoustic branch and wavenumber ring.

Case~C adds periodic contrast in both shear modulus and density. The resulting Bloch bands separate Bands~3 and~4 over the sampled Brillouin zone, unlike the path-specific branch separation in homogeneous Case~H. This difference is consistent with the positive complete-gap measure in Case~C and the absence of a complete gap in Case~H. The Case-C gap decreases from $2.5732$ on the $4\times4$ mesh to $1.9179$ on the $64\times64$ mesh and remains unconverged. Because the cases use different material and discretization settings, the comparison is interpretive rather than a controlled parameter sweep; the coarse-mesh Case-C width is not a continuum estimate.

The homogeneous mesh study gives an empirical rate $p=4.17$ over the $4^2$, $8^2$, and $16^2$ levels. The $32^2$ result is resolution-limited and excluded from the fit, so this rate is not a theoretical-order claim. All eight tabulated numerical consistency checks meet their stated criteria; the energy-flux/group-velocity identity, evaluated on closed-form Case-H harmonic fields, holds to $6.91\times10^{-10}$.

The classical AlN/BaTiO$_3$ calculation reproduces the Rytov transfer-matrix relation to $2.22\times10^{-16}$, although the comparison with the published B1 curves stays visual because tabulated source values are unavailable. The two gradient-elastic bilayer cases are reproductions rather than quantitative external validations, since their source parameter conventions are unresolved; no external-validation claim is made for those benchmarks.

For the transverse branch in Appendix~\ref{app:asymptotics}, gradient stiffness raises the phase speed at high wavenumber when micro-inertia is absent. With micro-inertia, the $k^2$ contribution in the numerator of $v_p^2$ is balanced asymptotically by the $k^2$ inertial term in its denominator, leaving a finite limit set by the ratio of the gradient-stiffness and micro-inertia lengths. This is a mechanism-specific result for the branch and constitutive model studied, not a universal bound. More broadly, the model is linear, local, isothermal, and two-dimensional under plane-strain assumptions; it excludes nonlinear gradient effects, dissipation, piezoelectric coupling, and three-dimensional polarization modes.
