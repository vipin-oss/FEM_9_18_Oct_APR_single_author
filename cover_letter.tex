\textbf{Subject:} Original research article submission

Dear Editor,

I submit the single-author manuscript entitled \textbf{\textquotedblleft Directional Wave Dispersion and Energy-Flow Steering in Two-Dimensional Strain-Gradient Elastic Solids\textquotedblright} for consideration as an original research article in the \textit{International Journal of Solids and Structures}.

\textbf{Fit with the journal's aims and scope.}

The journal publishes original research on the mechanics of solids and structures, including fundamental mathematical and numerical approaches, micro- and nano-mechanics, and architected and lattice materials. The submitted study addresses the elastodynamics of a higher-order elastic solid rather than band-gap optimisation alone. It examines how the orientation and aspect ratio of intrinsic material lengths alter the dispersion relation and the direction of elastic energy transport. The governing model, its higher-order finite-element formulation, and the group-velocity analysis concern the mechanical response of solids with microstructural length scales. The separate periodic-composite example connects the continuum model to wave propagation in a material-contrast microstructure. Thus, the paper directly addresses size-dependent constitutive response and wave mechanics in solids, which fall within the journal's stated scope.

\textbf{Novelty and scientific contribution.}

The principal contribution is a joint analysis of directional strain-gradient stiffness, independent gradient micro-inertia, and energy-flow steering in a two-dimensional Bloch setting. A rotated ellipsoidal characteristic-length tensor is incorporated into a local Mindlin Form-II model, while displacement and derivative degrees of freedom are coupled consistently across periodic boundaries using a conforming Bogner--Fox--Schmit element. The results separate two physically different effects: a homogeneous solid develops direction-dependent reduced-zone spectral separation but no complete Bragg gap, whereas a periodically contrasted composite shows a complete gap on the computed meshes. The homogeneous acoustic group velocity deviates from the wave vector by up to $2.78^\circ$; rotating the length tensor redirects the steering lobes without substantially changing their peak deviation. Analytical limits and numerical consistency checks support these findings. The composite-gap width is not yet mesh-converged, and this limitation is stated explicitly. The novelty lies in the coupled mechanics and the distinction between these spectral mechanisms, not in claiming that the established BFS interpolation is a new general-purpose finite element algorithm.
