\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The directional separations in Case~H arise from the anisotropic gradient stiffness of a homogeneous continuum. At fixed volume-equivalent length, changing aspect ratio shifts the two bounding frequencies by different amounts. Rotation changes which propagation directions are most affected. Even where $\Delta_{GX}>0$, the complete-gap measure is negative ($\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}}\le -0.3235$). Material contrast is absent, and the 42 evaluated configurations sample a finite design grid. These results concern reduced-zone dispersion, not a Bragg attenuation gap or a continuously optimized design.

The group-velocity calculation supplies a second view of the same anisotropy. On the acoustic branch at $\bar{k}=0.5$, rotation moves the steering lobes around the constant-wavenumber ring while their maximum angular deviation changes little. The local normal to a constant-frequency contour gives $\bmv_g=\nabla_{\bmk}\omega$. Its direction need not coincide with $\bmk$; this is the mechanism behind the computed steering. The result is specific to the branch and wavenumber ring tested.

Case~C behaves differently because its shear modulus and density vary periodically. Bands~3 and~4 remain separated over the sampled Brillouin zone, but the complete-gap width falls from $2.5732$ on the $4\times4$ mesh to $1.9179$ on the $64\times64$ mesh. Further refinement is needed before the width can be treated as a continuum value. The homogeneous and composite cases also use different material and numerical settings, so the comparison identifies different mechanisms rather than isolating one parameter.

Numerical checks support the implementation within these limits. The homogeneous mesh study gives an empirical rate of $p=4.17$ on its first three resolutions; the $32^2$ result lies at the numerical floor. Eight consistency checks meet their stated criteria, including a $6.91\times10^{-10}$ energy-flux/group-velocity residual evaluated using closed-form Case-H fields. The classical AlN/BaTiO$_3$ transfer-matrix relation agrees to $2.22\times10^{-16}$, but comparisons with published bilayer band plots remain qualitative where source data or parameter conventions are missing. In the transverse-branch asymptotics, micro-inertia balances the gradient-stiffness contribution and produces a finite high-wavenumber phase speed; other branches are not covered by that result. The continuum model itself is linear, local, isothermal and two-dimensional. Dissipation, nonlinear effects, piezoelectric coupling and three-dimensional polarization modes have not been included.
