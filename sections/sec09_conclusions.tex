
\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The two-dimensional Mindlin Form-II Bloch-wave analysis leads to the following main findings:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The conforming Bogner--Fox--Schmit element applies the same Bloch phase to displacement and derivative degrees of freedom. Analytical limiting cases and numerical checks support the implementation.

    \item In homogeneous Case~H, the dispersion branches change with the orientation and aspect ratio of the characteristic-length tensor. Directional reduced-zone separations are observed, but no complete band gap is found.

    \item At a fixed ellipse area, the directional separation does not vary monotonically with the aspect ratio. The largest sampled value is $\Delta_{GX}=0.0677$ at $\theta=0^\circ$ and $\mathrm{AR}=7$.

    \item Rotating the characteristic-length tensor changes the direction of acoustic energy flow. The maximum group-velocity deviation is approximately $2.78^\circ$ and varies only slightly over the sampled orientations.

    \item The periodic epoxy--YBCO composite exhibits a gap between Bands~3 and~4 on every computed mesh. However, the gap width decreases under mesh refinement, and convergence has not been established.

    \item At high wavenumbers, gradient micro-inertia limits the transverse-branch phase speed to $0.3162$ in normalised units. Without gradient micro-inertia, the phase speed grows without bound.
\end{itemize}

These results describe directional elastic-wave transport and steering in strain-gradient solids. The homogeneous model isolates the influence of tensor orientation without introducing periodic material contrast. The composite model separately examines band-gap formation due to periodic heterogeneity. Further mesh-convergence studies and material-specific calibration are required before quantitative predictions of the composite band gap can be made.
